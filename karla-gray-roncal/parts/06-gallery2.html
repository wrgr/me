Plate XI2018

Rung 4 · a test in the patient’s own hands

Acceptability and usage of the PROSe mobile system among pediatric patients with chronic headache

The migraine app she had project-managed in 2015 — designed through a think-tank that put human factors engineers, graphic artists, neurologists and an actual migraine patient in one room — tested on children with chronic headache. Note what the study measures: not headache, but acceptability and usage.

The premise. An instrument nobody uses records nothing. Adherence is not a side issue in patient-reported outcomes; it is the measurement.

Plate XIII2021

Rung 4 · a test in the patient’s own hands · most cited

Association of disease severity and socioeconomic status in Black and White Americans with multiple sclerosis

Her most-cited work. Across 1,214 Black American and 7,530 White American participants in the MS PATHS cohort, measured on a tablet the patient operates themselves, the differences are present after adjustment for education, employment and insurance — and a subgroup analysis reached neighbourhood deprivation through 9-digit ZIP codes, a deliberately harder instrument than asking.

Severe vs mild disability OR 1.47 (1.18–1.86) — while moderate vs mild is 1.04, essentially null: the gap opens only at the severe end. Processing speed −5.06 correct · walking +0.66 s · dexterity +2.11 s · thalamus −0.77 mL · cortical grey matter −30.63 mL. The sting: lower neighbourhood status tracked worse scores in White Americans; among Black Americans that association was much less clear. And the reversal: in healthy cohorts, Black Americans have greater brain volumes — so this is not a baseline difference being rediscovered.

Plate XV2024

Rung 5 · the body itself

An algorithm to predict new MRI lesions to support improved disease surveillance in multiple sclerosis

Both halves of her training in one author list: the Applied Physics Laboratory and the Department of Neurology, on the same paper. MS monitoring means repeated MRI on a schedule nobody has individualised. Using 1,131 patients from the Johns Hopkins MS Center, the model predicts who will show something new on their next scan.

Read the threshold, not the AUC. At 0.08 the model is tuned toward not missing lesions rather than toward looking accurate. That is a human-factors judgement about what a false negative costs a person, made inside a machine-learning paper.

Plate XVI2025

Rung 5 · the body itself

Association of serum neurofilament light chain deviation levels to new lesions on MRI in people with multiple sclerosis

Neurofilament light chain is a protein that leaks into the blood when nerve fibres are damaged. The question is whether a deviation from a person's own baseline level flags new lesions before a scheduled scan would find them — swapping an expensive, infrequent instrument for a cheap, frequent one.

The same move as the phone call, at molecular scale: increase the sampling rate of the question, and things you were missing become visible.

Toward a framework for providing equitable opportunities to activate STEM talent

Fifteen years of running the College Prep Program, written up as a systems-based approach — how to systematise and measure outreach programmes without eating the time that would otherwise go to the students. Identifying racist microaggressions in health system encounters with people participating in neurology care and research

The end of the ladder. Her 2021 paper concluded that the disparity survived every socioeconomic explanation it could measure, and pointed toward “other societal constructs (e.g., systemic racism)” as the place to look next. Plate XIX2026

Rung 7 · a conversation · first author

Increased contact with clinical trial participants may improve confirmed multiple sclerosis relapse detection

First author, and the cleanest statement of the whole method. In the VIDAMS vitamin D trial, coordinators contacted participants between visits and asked specific questions about symptoms. Confirmation was not soft: a blinded neurologist had to document worsening on the disability scale at an unscheduled visit.

Nothing biological happens at the moment of a phone call. The relapses in the “before” windows were happening too, and going uncounted — and confirmed relapse is the endpoint phase 3 MS trials are powered on. The authors keep it to an association (“temporally associated”, “may improve”), and so do we.
