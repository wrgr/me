The atlas.

One plate per work, in ladder order. Each illustration is drawn from what that publication actually does — not a stock icon — and the colour is its thread. Where a source cannot be linked, the plate says why rather than leaving a dead end.

Plate I2008

Rung 1 · a console

Role of Watch Section Task Analysis in the Development of Advanced Processor Builds

Her first publication, under her earlier surname, Roncal. New sonar processing was reaching submarines faster than anyone could say whether it helped, because the tests measured the system and not the watch section using it. She helped build the quantitative and qualitative test measures for assessing crew and console as one object.

The move. The unit of analysis becomes operator + machine. Every later paper on this page inherits that choice.

T. Roy*, K. Roncal*, et al. · Submarine Technology Symposium, JHU/APL · no public record: the symposium is classified, so no DOI exists or ever will

Plate II2010

Rung 1 · a console

APB Technologies to Support Multi-Mission Submarine Maritime Theater Operations

By now she is assistant programme manager for Advanced Processor Build and mentoring a team of ten analysts. The recurring job, in her own CV's words, is leading the introduction of Operator Machine Interfaces into the Navy's advanced development process — so that new capability has to prove itself against the person who will use it, not only on a bench.

The phrase. “Operator machine interface” is her actual job title in 2010. It is also, this page argues, what she has worked on ever since.

K. Roncal, G. Banta, et al. · Submarine Technology Symposium, JHU/APL · no public record: classified symposium

Plate III2011

Rung 2 · a ward

Task analysis of Intensive Care Units at Johns Hopkins

The hinge of the career, and almost invisible because it produced a mock-up room rather than a paper. She takes the submarine methodology — watch the operators, map the task, find the seams where technology and people fail to meet — and points it at an ICU, to inform design across multiple stakeholders and machine interfaces.

The date. 2011. Eight years before she finishes medical school, her method is already inside a hospital.

K. Gray-Roncal, human factors engineer, JHU/APL · internal project; described in her CV, no publication

Plate IV2015

Rung 2 · a ward

Pressure Ulcer Solutions Project — a medical lexicon for engineers

Systems engineers were asked to solve pressure ulcers — injuries that kill people in hospitals — and could not read the clinical literature. Her deliverable was a lexicon guide introducing non-clinical staff to medical language, so that the stakeholders could hear each other.

The literal version. Of everything in this record, this is the most on-the-nose: an interface between two systems, shipped as a document.

K. Gray-Roncal, JHU/APL · internal project; described in her CV

Plate V2015

Rung 2 · a ward · the exception

Risk of gonadal failure in pediatric blood cancer patients

Written as a Georgetown medical student: a review of fertility loss in children treated for blood cancer, and what might be done to preserve reproductive options in survivors. This is the publication that fits the theory least well, and we are not going to bend it. You can call a late effect an unmeasured outcome, but that is a stretch, and a stretch is worth admitting.

Why it stays. A theory with no exceptions is usually a theory that has been trimmed to fit.

L. Antognoli, N. Apseloff, J. Lu, K. Roncal · Current Opinions in Sexual Development and Reproduction 7(M1), Georgetown University Medical Center · student-run journal; no DOI registered, not indexed anywhere

Plate VI2018

Rung 3 · a benchmark

IARPA MICrONS — Human–Machine Baseline Task Lead

She led the design and implementation of the paradigm for comparing human and algorithm performance inside MICrONS — IARPA's programme to close the gap between human analysts and automated pattern recognition by reverse-engineering the brain's own algorithms from a densely mapped cubic millimetre of cortex.

The division of labour. Her half was not the algorithm. It was making sure the humans it would be measured against had been measured properly.

Plate VII2022

Rung 3 · a benchmark · keystone

A framework for rigorous evaluation of human performance in human and machine learning comparison studies

The keystone. Machine learning papers routinely claim to beat people without saying how many people, who they were, or how they were recruited — the paper titled “Surpassing human-level performance on ImageNet classification” rested that claim on one annotator. The framework sets out how to build a human baseline that means something: control for memory, language and prior experience, match the trials, and run it like a psychology study, ethics review included.

134 participants · 200 matched trials · 45% easy / 45% hard / 10% attention checks. Drop from five examples to one and the algorithm falls 92.7% → 54.4%; the humans go 94.6% → 95.8%. But it cuts both ways — the paper warns equally about baselines that flatter humans and make an algorithm look worse than it is.

H. P. Cowley, M. Natter, K. Gray-Roncal, R. E. Rhodes, E. C. Johnson, N. Drenkow, T. M. Shead, F. S. Chance, B. Wester, W. Gray-Roncal · Scientific Reports 12, 5444 · 10.1038/s41598-022-08078-3 ↗ · CC BY 4.0

Plate VIII2022

Rung 3 · a benchmark

Author Correction to the framework paper

A short correction: one citation was reassigned to two more appropriate sources, and a further example of a well-executed human–algorithm comparison was added. It is included here rather than tidied away because a paper arguing for rigorous evaluation, which then publicly fixes its own reference list, is being consistent rather than embarrassed.

A small irony we checked and are reporting. The correction's added sentence dates its new reference to 2009; the paper it names is from 2019. That slip has never itself been corrected.

Plate IX2018

Rung 3 · a benchmark

The College Prep Program at APL: an experiential model to help high-achieving, underserved students trailblaze and achieve success

She co-founded this all-volunteer summer programme in 2009 and ran it for eleven years; she is first and corresponding author here, with a second affiliation to her own Preparation Meets Opportunity Foundation. Its premise is a measurement claim rather than a charitable one: there are students with the desire and academic potential to excel who will not be identified, because the instruments that select for college are also quietly measuring mentoring and resources.

220+ students mentored; nearly all on track to earn a degree. Recognised by a Howard County Volunteer of the Year award in 2014.

K. Gray-Roncal, L. Huynh, T. Kolarik, M. Roncal, M. A. Saunders, W. Gray-Roncal · IEEE ISEC 2018, pp. 90–97 · 10.1109/ISECON.2018.8340511 ↗ · IEEE, closed access

Plate X2019

Rung 3 · a benchmark

An accessible, distributed, technology-based approach for student and mentor engagement

The scaling problem for the College Prep model. Mentorship works, and mentorship does not travel. This paper describes the low-cost, extensible ecosystem they built over ten programme cycles to keep students, mentors and leadership connected across distance, adapting corporate collaboration tools to an all-volunteer outreach setting.

The pattern. An operator–machine interface applied to the one relationship everyone assumed required a shared room.

L. Huynh, K. Gray-Roncal, M. Roncal, W. Gray-Roncal · IEEE ISEC 2019, pp. 142–148 · 10.1109/ISECon.2019.8882110 ↗ · IEEE, closed access
