Coda

Her own version.

The honest finding first: we could not locate a single sentence, anywhere in public, in which Karla Gray-Roncal states a through-line for her own work. No manifesto, no essay, no lab-page paragraph. The closest thing that exists is four lines under the heading Research Focus in her curriculum vitae.

Research focus — her words, verbatim, from her CV
  • Clinical trials addressing detection of relapses in MS and use of biomarkers for disease monitoring
  • Technology integration in neurological care and research practices
  • Healthcare disparities and access in multiple sclerosis
  • Mentoring and education of next generation trailblazers and leaders

Read as a list, that is four jobs, which is exactly how a CV is built to read. Read as one sentence, it is: detect what is being missed, build the instrument that detects it, check who the instrument is missing, and go find the people it never counted.

One word in that list is worth following. Trailblazers appears in her research focus; it appears in the title of her 2018 paper about underserved students; and it appears again in the 2024 framework, which defines trailblazing students as those with both high potential and significant barriers. It is her word, used the same way across seventeen years and two disciplines — a person whose ability is real and whose obstacles are also real, and whom the standard instrument will therefore score wrong.

She holds the Sylvia Lawry Physician Fellowship. Sylvia Lawry founded the National Multiple Sclerosis Society in 1946 after placing a classified advertisement in a newspaper the year before, asking anyone who had recovered from multiple sclerosis to write to her. Around fifty people replied. None had recovered. The field she now works in was founded by somebody deciding to ask a question systematically and count the answers — and the paper that carries her own name first says the same thing, seventy-eight years later, with a confidence interval attached.

Where the numbers come from

Every quantitative claim traces to a primary source: PubMed and Europe PMC records, publisher full text, Crossref, medRxiv, OpenAlex, the IARPA programme page, and the National MS Society's own published grants list.

Citation counts are OpenAlex, retrieved August 2026, and will drift. She has no Google Scholar profile, and her ORCID (0009-0006-3512-7978) is registered but empty, so counts differ noticeably between indexes — the 2022 framework paper shows 25 on Semantic Scholar and 56 on OpenAlex. We used one source throughout rather than picking the flattering number per paper.

Errors we found and did not repeat

  • Third-party records for the 2022 Scientific Reports paper circulate a wrong DOI and PMCID, lifted from its own reference list. The correct DOI is 10.1038/s41598-022-08078-3; PMCID PMC8971503.
  • Table 1 of the 2021 Neurology paper prints “220 (28.2)” for male sex among Black American participants; 220 of 1,214 is 18.1%. We have used counts rather than that percentage and quote no sex distribution from it.
  • The 2022 Author Correction dates a newly added reference to 2009; the paper is from 2019. That slip has never itself been corrected.
  • A widely-surfaced figure of “$195,500, 2021–2024” for her fellowship belongs to a different Hopkins fellow. Hers is $225,000, 1 July 2023 – 30 June 2026, per the Society's own grants list.

What we could not verify

  • Four items exist only in her CV — the 2011 ICU task analysis, the 2015 medical lexicon, the 2018 MICrONS baseline role, and her equal-contribution claims on two papers. The CV was supplied to us directly; we have marked every such item as CV-sourced rather than presenting it as independently confirmed.
  • The two submarine papers cannot be checked by anyone. The JHU/APL Submarine Technology Symposium is classified and its proceedings are not published. That is a structural fact, not a failed search.
  • Four conference abstracts could not be read — ACTRIMS 2025 (microaggressions), ANA 2018 (PROSe), CMSC and ANA 2025 (neurofilament light chain). The society supplements are paywalled or block automated access. Their titles come from her CV. The microaggressions abstract is the one document we most wanted and could not get.
  • Her SXSW 2022 speaker bio is archived but unreachable from here, and the 2015 Georgetown student-journal review is indexed nowhere.
  • Sylvia Lawry's advertisement is well attested as 1945 with about fifty replies; sources vary slightly on the count.
