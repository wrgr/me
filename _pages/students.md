<section id="premise">
  <p class="prose">
    Every program on this page starts from the same conviction — that
    <strong>a demonstrated output is harder to inherit than a proxy</strong>.
    Scaffold people so they can do real work inside the established system, then
    let the demonstration decide. Including the right people means finding them
    first — and judging that accurately means seeing the work.
  </p>
  <p class="prose">
    You do not need to arrive knowing all of this. No one carries every tool;
    this is team work, and what you already bring is the place you start.
  </p>
</section>

<section id="programs">
  <div class="kicker"><span class="eyebrow">Pathways</span></div>
  <h2>The programs</h2>

  <h3>CIRCUIT</h3>
  <p class="prose">
    Cohort-based Integrated Research Community for Undergraduate Innovation and
    Trailblazing — six cycles at APL from 2017 to 2023, built for students from
    <em>trailblazing</em> backgrounds: first-generation, low-income, and those
    with limited prior access to research. Cohorts contributed to reconstructions
    published in <em>Nature</em>.
  </p>
  <p class="prose">Eight pillars structure the model:</p>
  <ul class="tight">
    <li><strong>Holistic recruiting</strong> that does not screen on credentials a trailblazer's background would not have generated</li>
    <li><strong>Mission engagement</strong>, so trainees see why the technical skill they are acquiring matters</li>
    <li><strong>Targeted training</strong> built around the project's immediate needs</li>
    <li><strong>Leadership development</strong></li>
    <li><strong>Integrated assessment</strong> — learning and confidence run through feedback loops per module, not a summative pass/fail</li>
    <li><strong>Diverse mentorship</strong></li>
    <li><strong>Academic partnerships</strong> that route the cohort across institutions</li>
    <li><strong>Career empowerment</strong> that sustains capability beyond the program</li>
  </ul>

  <h3>MERIT and COMPASS</h3>
  <p class="prose">
    <strong>MERIT</strong> — Mentoring Exceptional Researchers to Innovate and
    Thrive — generalizes the CIRCUIT model into a structured, scalable research
    training framework. <strong>COMPASS</strong> is the layer of targeted mentor
    interventions that operates MERIT inside a specific institution, designed to
    help students surface the tacit and explicit knowledge gaps that make up the
    hidden curriculum of research practice. Both are documented as JHU/APL
    whitepapers and program documentation; CIRCUIT is the one with a
    peer-reviewed program description.
  </p>

  <h3>PISTA</h3>
  <p class="prose">
    Perceptual Inference System for Tactical Agentic AI — a ten-week summer
    incubator sponsored by DTRA JSTO in 2025. Nine student fellows shipped a
    working CBRNE decision-support prototype to agency leadership, through three
    full redesigns.
  </p>

  <h3>College Prep Program at APL</h3>
  <p class="prose">
    An all-volunteer summer program he co-founded in 2009 and still co-directs,
    supporting underserved students through academics, applications, essays, and
    financial aid, with longitudinal support afterward. More than 90% of
    participants go on track for four-year degrees, on 500+ volunteer hours a
    year.
  </p>
</section>

<section id="lens">
  <div class="kicker"><span class="eyebrow">Graduate study</span></div>
  <h2>LENS</h2>
  <p class="prose">
    <strong>Learning Engineering for Next-Generation Systems</strong> is a
    concentration within the Johns Hopkins M.Ed. in Learning Design and
    Technology — a 10-course, 30-credit online degree — the program directed by
    James Diamond at the School of Education. Gray-Roncal leads the
    concentration's development.
  </p>
  <p class="prose">
    It welcomes two audiences equally: education and training professionals
    moving toward systems practice, and engineers and technical professionals
    moving toward learning design. Five competencies anchor it —
    <strong>systems analysis</strong>, <strong>iterative development</strong>,
    <strong>human–system collaboration</strong>, <strong>test and
    evaluation</strong>, and <strong>navigating sociotechnical
    constraints</strong> — and the capstone runs against a real operational
    problem.
  </p>
