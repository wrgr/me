Plates I – XIX

Every publication, drawn and explained

One illustration per paper, drawn from what that paper actually does rather than from its field. Numbers are quoted from the papers themselves; anything I could not verify is marked as such rather than smoothed over.

Plate I 2010 · a cell

A Mathematical Model of the Primary T Cell Response with Contraction Governed by Adaptive Regulatory T Cells

S. N. Wilson, P. Lee, D. Levy · IFMBE Proceedings 32:209–212 · 10.1007/978-3-642-14998-6_53

Her first publication, from the Southern Biomedical Engineering Conference held at her own university. An immune response has three acts — it expands, it contracts, it leaves memory behind — and the question is what performs act two. The proposal is that contraction is not decay but regulation: a subpopulation that adapts to the response it is suppressing.

  • 3 authors, including Peter Lee at Stanford and her advisor Doron Levy
  • 1 citation on Crossref — and it is the seed of the two papers that follow

Plate II 2012 · a cell

A Mathematical Model of the Enhancement of Tumor Vaccine Efficacy by Immunotherapy

S. Wilson, D. Levy · Bulletin of Mathematical Biology 74(7) · 10.1007/s11538-012-9722-4

Her most-cited paper. TGF-β is an immunoregulatory protein that helps tumours evade the immune system; blocking it alone does little. The model — tumour size, TGF-β concentration, activated cytotoxic effector cells and regulatory T cells — was built to reproduce a specific mouse experiment, and does, including the part where only the combination eradicates the tumour.

  • 4 coupled state variables; solved in MATLAB R2010a with ode23
  • Tumour growth rate 0.1946 day⁻¹ and carrying capacity 369 mm², both fitted to the control data of Terabe et al. (2009)
  • Vaccine modelled as 5,000 TGF-β-resistant effector cells added on day 3; simulations run day 3 → day 30
  • Initial condition assumes ~100 activated effector cells at the tumour site
  • 88 citations — more than the rest of her pre-2020 work combined

Plate III 2013 · a cell

Functional Switching and Stability of Regulatory T Cells

S. Wilson, D. Levy · Bulletin of Mathematical Biology 75(10) · 10.1007/s11538-013-9875-9

The pivotal small paper. The immunology literature disagreed about whether regulatory T cells can lose their regulatory function and become immunostimulatory. Rather than assume an answer, she made the transition a modelled mechanism — in an ODE version and in a more biologically detailed delay-differential version — and asked what it buys you.

  • Models with switching still show the normal expansion, contraction and memory phases, but are more robust to the assumed precursor frequency of T cells
  • The paper identifies a biologically testable range for the switching parameter — a modelling result posed as an experiment
  • Conclusion: regulatory T cell switching “may play a key role in controlling immune contraction”

Plate IV 2013 · a mouse

Theoretical investigation of the efficacy of antiangiogenic drugs combined to chemotherapy in xenografted mice

F. Lignet, S. Benzekry, S. Wilson, F. Billy, O. Saut, M. Tod, B. You et al. · Journal of Theoretical Biology 320 · 10.1016/j.jtbi.2012.12.013

A multi-scale model that couples a molecular description of VEGF signalling to a tissue-scale description of tumour growth. Its contribution is a definition: an index of vasculature quality, set by the balance of stable to unstable vessels inside the tumour mass, which converts a vague clinical intuition into an optimisation target.

  • 12 authors across the INRIA, Bordeaux and Lyon groups
  • The question posed is explicitly one of timing: how long to wait after the antiangiogenic before delivering chemotherapy
  • Framed as groundwork for translating preclinical scheduling into clinical studies

Plate V 2015 · a mouse · the method keystone

Modeling and predicting optimal treatment scheduling between the antiangiogenic drug sunitinib and irinotecan in preclinical settings

S. Wilson, M. Tod, A. Ouerdani, A. Emde, Y. Yarden, B. Ribba et al. · CPT: Pharmacometrics & Systems Pharmacology 4(12) · 10.1002/psp4.12045

The paper where the method and the philosophy are the same object. A system of nonlinear ODEs for tumour growth, vasculature generation and antiangiogenic treatment, fitted to a large longitudinal mouse dataset — and then, crucially, validated against a follow-up experiment it had not seen.

  • 1,371 longitudinal tumour size measurements in 105 colorectal tumour-bearing mice
  • Parameters estimated “either using a mixed-effect approach or by nonlinear least squares”, depending on the dataset
  • A log-likelihood ratio test supports a synergistic interaction between sunitinib and irinotecan
  • Simulations were then compared against a follow-up preclinical experiment — the model had predictive value for the timing window, not just descriptive fit
  • Note: her CV lists this under an earlier title (“…combination scheduling between antiangiogenic drug and chemotherapy”); the published title names the two drugs. The published version is used here.

Plate VI 2015 · a sleeper

Effects of Thermoregulation on Human Sleep Patterns: A Mathematical Model of Sleep–Wake Cycles with REM–NREM Subcircuit

S. Bañuelos, J. Best, G. Huguet, A. Prieto-Langarica, P. B. Pyzza, M. H. Schmidt, S. Wilson · The IMA Volumes in Mathematics and its Applications 158 · 10.1007/978-1-4939-2782-1_6

The first of the sleep papers, from a collaboration of six mathematicians that would keep going for five years. Existing sleep–wake models treated sleep as a single state. This one adds the REM/NREM subcircuit, so that the internal architecture of a night is produced by the model rather than assumed by it.

  • 7 authors, including sleep physiologist Markus H. Schmidt — the domain expert inside a mathematics collaboration
  • Published in an IMA volume on Applications of Dynamical Systems in Biology and Medicine

Plate VII 2020 · a sleeper

Modeling the long term effects of thermoregulation on human sleep

S. Bañuelos, J. Best, G. Huguet, A. Prieto-Langarica, P. B. Pyzza, S. Wilson · Journal of Theoretical Biology 494:110208 · 10.1016/j.jtbi.2020.110208

The follow-up, five years later, extends the model with equations for the metabolic processes controlling thermoregulation during sleep — motivated by a theory that REM sleep is precisely when energy normally spent on temperature control is diverted elsewhere. The model then answers, quantitatively, how a sleeper adjusts their stage progression when the room is not thermoneutral.

  • Simulations cover both a single night and several consecutive nights — the “long term” of the title
  • Stated conclusion: “including the effects of temperature is a vital component of modeling sleep”
  • The illustration’s fragmentation pattern is schematic — it shows the paper’s claim, not digitised output. Per-stage durations were not extracted; the full text is behind a paywall.

Plate VIII 2018 · an animal in a troop

How Disease Risks Can Impact the Evolution of Social Behaviors and Emergent Population Organization

N. D. Williams, H. Z. Brooks, M. E. Hohn, C. R. Price, A. E. Radunskaya, S. S. Sindi, S. N. Wilson, N. H. Fefferman · Association for Women in Mathematics Series 14, 31–46 · 10.1007/978-3-319-98083-6_2

The first output of the workshop project. If being social means being exposed, then infection pressure ought to be one of the forces that shaped animal societies in the first place — which reframes social structure from a fixed setting into an evolved outcome.

  • 8 authors, all women, from a Mathematical Biosciences Institute Women Advancing Mathematical Biology workshop (March 2017) where Wilson was a group co-lead
  • Published in the Understanding Complex Biological Systems with Mathematics volume of the AWM Series
  • 3 citations — the ideas travelled through the 2020 paper instead

Plate IX 2018 · an animal in a troop

Mathematical Analysis of the Impact of Social Structure on Ectoparasite Load in Allogrooming Populations

H. Z. Brooks, M. E. Hohn, C. R. Price, A. E. Radunskaya, S. S. Sindi, N. D. Williams, S. N. Wilson, N. H. Fefferman · Association for Women in Mathematics Series 14, 47–61 · 10.1007/978-3-319-98083-6_3

The companion chapter, running the comparison the other way: fix the grooming behaviour, vary the social structure, and see where the parasite burden ends up. It is the analysis that the 2020 paper turns into a full simulation study.

  • Same 8 authors, adjacent pages in the same volume — the pair is really one piece of work
  • 0 Crossref citations. It is a load-bearing step in the argument that nobody has cited, which is the ordinary fate of chapters
  • The bar heights above are schematic, taking their ordering from the 2020 paper’s published results. The chapter’s own figures were not accessible.

Plate X 2020 · an animal in a troop

How Emergent Social Patterns in Allogrooming Combat Parasitic Infections

S. N. Wilson, S. S. Sindi, H. Z. Brooks, M. E. Hohn, C. R. Price, A. E. Radunskaya, N. D. Williams, N. H. Fefferman · Frontiers in Ecology and Evolution 8:54 · 10.3389/fevo.2020.00054

Wilson is first author. Fifty animals, each grooming five neighbours; every step they drop their two lowest-ranked partners and pick two new ones. Grooming both removes parasites from the groomee and moves parasites along the edge. The only thing that varies between conditions is the rule for choosing partners.

  • N = 50 hosts, 5 connections each, 2 partners swapped per step, 300 steps, 100 trials per condition, in MATLAB R2019a
  • Network organises for the first 150 steps; parasites are introduced to a single host at step 150
  • Under degree-preference, exactly 3 core nodes end up with 49 groomers each; every other node averages fewer than 5
  • Grooming efficiencies tested: g = 0, 0.001, 0.004, 0.008. At g = 0.004 only the random-affiliation population extinguishes the outbreak
  • Other parameters: max load m = 1, reproduction factor r = 0.01, bidirectional spread factor α = 0.15, transmission taken as 10% of a host’s neighbours’ average load
  • Discussion: fighting an endemic parasite “requires a fundamentally different social structure than preventing the spread of a novel invasive threat”
