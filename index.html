Principal Research Engineer at the Johns Hopkins University Applied Physics Laboratory. Two decades of systems engineering across defense, space, biomedical, and learning systems — DARPA-, IARPA-, NIH-, and DoD-sponsored — bridging computational neuroscience, AI, and large-scale data infrastructure, and building the programs that grow the people who do this work.

The blend is the point

The through-line is systems engineering plus measurement, applied wherever capability must be built — in a reconstruction pipeline, a research team, or a learner. The same discipline that maps a cubic millimeter of cortex also builds the archive that shares it, the metrics that judge it, and the mentoring programs that staff it.

Programs & mentoring

Nearly 500 students mentored

CIRCUIT was built as the workforce solution to IARPA MICrONS: cohorts of undergraduates, recruited for potential rather than pedigree, whose reconstructions contributed materially to results published in Nature. Co-author of the peer-reviewed CIRCUIT model paper (ASEE 2023).

The model generalized into MERIT and the COMPASS mentor layer, and traveled to new sponsors: PISTA (DTRA, Summer 2025) took nine fellows through ten weeks and three full redesigns of a CBRNE decision-support prototype.

On the learning-engineering side, he leads the development of the LENS concentration within the JHU M.Ed. in Learning Design and Technology (School of Education), the program directed by James Diamond — where "capability is a system parameter" is the working thesis of capabilitymatters.org.
